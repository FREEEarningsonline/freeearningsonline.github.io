How to Write a Midjourney v6 Prompt That Actually Works

By Nazim Mustafa · Updated October 10, 2026

Most beginners type a few random words into Midjourney and feel disappointed by the result. Version 6 follows instructions much more literally than older versions, so a clear structure matters more than fancy keywords. This guide explains the formula I use and why each part exists.

The 5-part prompt formula

  1. Subject: who or what is in the image. Be specific: "an elderly fisherman mending a net" beats "a man".
  2. Setting: where it happens, such as a foggy harbor at dawn.
  3. Lighting: soft window light, golden hour, neon night glow. Lighting changes mood more than any other word.
  4. Style and camera: documentary photo, 35mm lens, shallow depth of field, or flat vector illustration.
  5. Parameters: aspect ratio, stylize and version.

A full example

an elderly fisherman mending a net on a wooden pier, foggy harbor at dawn, soft diffused light, documentary photo, 35mm lens, shallow depth of field --ar 3:2 --v 6

Each phrase answers one question the model would otherwise guess. When you remove the lighting phrase, the image usually becomes flat and generic.

Parameters you will use most

  • --ar 16:9 for widescreen, --ar 9:16 for phone wallpapers and Reels.
  • --stylize 50 keeps results close to your words; higher values add Midjourney's own artistic taste.
  • --no text, watermark removes unwanted elements.

Common mistakes

  • Keyword stuffing. Writing "8k, ultra, masterpiece, best quality" ten times does little in v6. Describe the scene instead.
  • Too many subjects. Three characters plus a complex background often confuse the layout. Start with one subject.
  • Changing everything at once. Change one element per attempt so you learn what caused the difference.

How to improve a result

Generate four images, pick the closest, and use the variation option. If the face or hands look wrong, simplify the pose in your prompt. If colors feel dull, add a lighting or color-palette phrase such as "warm amber tones".
